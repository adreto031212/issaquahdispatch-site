The latest in Central Issaquah

November 12, 2026

Tonight at 6:30, the Planning Policy Commission takes its first look at draft rules for through-block connections, the walkways that cut between buildings on Central Issaquah's big blocks. The draft limits the requirement to new development in the Regional Growth Center, sets one standard: eight feet wide with five feet of landscaping on each side, and maps fixed locations in place of today's every-300-feet rule. Council Chambers, 135 E. Sunset Way, or online. Agenda packet

November 5, 2026

Gilman Boulevard doubles as a walking tour of edible landscaping: about 25 varieties of plants line both sides of the boulevard, bearing everything from apples to nuts through spring, summer and fall. The city's one rule: take what you will use and leave enough for others. Tour page

October 29, 2026

Gilman Boulevard doubles as a walking tour of edible landscaping: about 25 varieties of plants line both sides of the boulevard, bearing everything from apples to nuts through spring, summer and fall. October 22, 2026

An 8-story apartment building is proposed at 1550 Newport Way NW: about 206 apartments and 244 parking stalls in roughly 326,000 square feet. The city hosts a community meeting Thursday, October 29, at 6 PM in the Steelhead Room, 235 1st Ave. SE, or online by Teams, where the applicant presents and residents can ask questions. Application materials are at issaquahwa.gov/development. Meeting notice

October 15, 2026

Pickering Barn, 1730 10th Ave. NW, hosts the Community Clothing Closet's free Winter Clothing Fest: donation day Monday, October 26, 1 to 5 PM (clean coats, sweaters, jeans, warm hats and gloves), and a free shopping day Wednesday, October 28, 10 AM to 3 PM. Donations are taken on donation days only. Donation day · Shopping day

Questions people ask

Is this a real estate newsletter?

No. It is a Issaquah newsletter that happens to know the housing market. Three of the four sections are about living here. The fourth tells you what homes are selling for, because in Issaquah that is news too.

Who writes it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publisher. Every item comes from Issaquah's own public record (council agendas, permit notices, school district and transit updates, parks calendars) and links its source. It is researched and written with AI tools, and an editor reads every issue before it goes out.

How often, and is it really free?

Once a week, Thursday at 6 AM, about five minutes to read. Free, no paywall, unsubscribe in one click at the bottom of any issue.

Who owns it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publishing company. The newsletter is free, and no sponsor buys coverage. Our standards.

What happens with my address?

It is used for one thing: a monthly estimate of what your home would sell for today. It is shared with a local real estate brokerage under the same ownership as our publisher, which may contact you about your home, and is never sold or posted. Remove it any time by replying to any issue.
