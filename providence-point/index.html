The latest in Providence Point

November 5, 2026

The East Lake Sammamish Trail runs along the lake west of the neighborhood: 11 paved miles, 12 feet wide with gravel shoulders, for walking, biking and every other non-motorized use, with a 15 mph limit. Issaquah access points are SE 51st, 56th and 62nd Streets and NW Gilman Boulevard. A 600-foot stretch in Sammamish near Louis Thompson Road NE is closed through the end of the year for culvert work, with no detour. King County Parks

October 29, 2026

The East Lake Sammamish Trail runs along the lake west of the neighborhood: 11 paved miles, 12 feet wide with gravel shoulders, for walking, biking and every other non-motorized use, with a 15 mph limit. Issaquah access points are SE 51st, 56th and 62nd Streets and NW Gilman Boulevard. Heading north, a 600-foot stretch in Sammamish between Louis Thompson Road NE and NE Inglewood Hill Road is closed through the end of the year for culvert work, with no detour. King County Parks

October 22, 2026

The Hard-to-Recycle collection is Saturday, October 31, 9 AM to 3 PM, at Eastside Catholic School, 232 228th Ave. SE, north up 228th. Up to six passenger tires off the rim are free; electronics, computers and TVs are taken, with a $5 cash charge per printer or copier. Fees are cash only, exact change, and no vehicles enter after 3 PM. Event page

October 15, 2026

Free drive-thru paint recycling runs Saturday, October 24, 9 AM to 3 PM, at Eastside Catholic School, 232 228th Ave. SE in Sammamish, north up 228th Avenue SE. Latex and oil-based paint, primers, stains and varnishes in containers of 5 gallons or less with original labels; no aerosols, thinners or solvents. Free, registration required. Event page

October 12, 2026

Lake Sammamish State Park warns of extra congestion at its entrances through late November because of the NW Sammamish Road closure. Inside the park, the Issaquah Creek and Tibbetts Creek bridges have been closed since March for erosion, with no repair date, and the Homestead Trail is closed. Washington State Parks

Questions people ask

Is this a real estate newsletter?

No. It is a Issaquah newsletter that happens to know the housing market. Three of the four sections are about living here. The fourth tells you what homes are selling for, because in Issaquah that is news too.

Who writes it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publisher. Every item comes from Issaquah's own public record (council agendas, permit notices, school district and transit updates, parks calendars) and links its source. It is researched and written with AI tools, and an editor reads every issue before it goes out.

How often, and is it really free?

Once a week, Thursday at 6 AM, about five minutes to read. Free, no paywall, unsubscribe in one click at the bottom of any issue.

Who owns it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publishing company. The newsletter is free, and no sponsor buys coverage. Our standards.

What happens with my address?

It is used for one thing: a monthly estimate of what your home would sell for today. It is shared with a local real estate brokerage under the same ownership as our publisher, which may contact you about your home, and is never sold or posted. Remove it any time by replying to any issue.
