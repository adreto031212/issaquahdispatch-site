The latest in Olde Town

November 12, 2026

Saturday, 9 AM to 2 PM, the Farm Fresh Market sets up at the Historic Shell Station, 232 Front St. N.: produce, flowers, music, and local food makers. It is back the Saturday after, November 21. The last StoryStroll of the season, picture-book pages along the downtown sidewalks, stays up through Sunday, November 15. Market · StoryStroll

November 5, 2026

The Open Mic Night at the Issaquah Train Depot, 78 1st Ave. NE, ran Wednesday, November 4. The Downtown Issaquah Association has hosted it on the first Wednesday of the month since 2008: sign-in at 6 PM, music from 6:30, and each performer gets two songs and 10 minutes. The next one is Wednesday, December 2. Event page

October 29, 2026

Open Mic Night returns to the Issaquah Train Depot, 78 1st Ave. NE, on Wednesday, November 4: sign-in at 6 PM, music from 6:30. The Downtown Issaquah Association has hosted it on the first Wednesday of the month since 2008. Each performer gets two songs and 10 minutes, so come tuned. Event page

October 22, 2026

The Darigold mural, painted in 1995, is mid-restoration. Evan Jones, one of its three original artists, and Claire Waterhouse have restored and sealed about 40%, with an ultraviolet coating to hold the colors; the remaining 60% waits for drier weather in May 2027. City of Issaquah

October 15, 2026

Issaquah's first Miyawaki pocket forest, 880 trees and shrubs at 190 NW Dogwood Street, gets a maintenance morning Saturday, October 24, 9 to 11 AM, with the Issaquah Alps Trails Club and Green Issaquah: weeding, mulching and watering. Registration is on the event page. Event page

October 12, 2026

The Darigold mural, first painted in 1995, is being restored by original artist Evan Jones with Claire Waterhouse. About 40% is done and sealed; the rest waits for May 2027 because of the weather. The Front Street and 2nd Avenue lift-station upgrade is due to finish in November. Mural · Lift station

Questions people ask

Is this a real estate newsletter?

No. It is a Issaquah newsletter that happens to know the housing market. Three of the four sections are about living here. The fourth tells you what homes are selling for, because in Issaquah that is news too.

Who writes it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publisher. Every item comes from Issaquah's own public record (council agendas, permit notices, school district and transit updates, parks calendars) and links its source. It is researched and written with AI tools, and an editor reads every issue before it goes out.

How often, and is it really free?

Once a week, Thursday at 6 AM, about five minutes to read. Free, no paywall, unsubscribe in one click at the bottom of any issue.

Who owns it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publishing company. The newsletter is free, and no sponsor buys coverage. Our standards.

What happens with my address?

It is used for one thing: a monthly estimate of what your home would sell for today. It is shared with a local real estate brokerage under the same ownership as our publisher, which may contact you about your home, and is never sold or posted. Remove it any time by replying to any issue.
