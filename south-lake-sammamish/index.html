The latest in South Lake Sammamish

November 12, 2026

Sunday, 9 AM to noon, Green Issaquah plants native trees and shrubs at Timberlake Park, 4500 W. Lake Sammamish Parkway SE, to grow back forest habitat. Gloves, tools and the know-how are provided; reserve a spot online. Event page

November 5, 2026

Tonight, 6 to 7 PM, city staff and WSDOT hold a South Lake Sammamish open house at Tibbetts Creek Manor, 750 17th Ave. NW. Topics include fish-passage progress, the NW Sammamish Road walking and biking improvements, utility work ahead of road closures, and the coordinated 2027–2029 closure timeline for Newport Way, NW Sammamish Road and West Lake Sammamish Parkway. Event page

October 29, 2026

City staff and WSDOT hold a South Lake Sammamish open house Thursday, November 5, 6 to 7 PM, at Tibbetts Creek Manor, 750 17th Ave. NW. Topics: fish-passage progress, the NW Sammamish Road walking and biking improvements, utility work ahead of road closures, private construction, and the coordinated 2027–2029 closure timeline for Newport Way, NW Sammamish Road and West Lake Sammamish Parkway. Event page

October 22, 2026

Meerwood Park, 1.75 acres off 192nd Avenue SE in the Sammamish Cove area, has a basketball court, tennis courts, play equipment, picnic tables and an open lawn. Open dawn to dusk. Park page

October 15, 2026

Next on the city's list after this fall's road work: bicycle and pedestrian improvements on NW Sammamish Road between 193rd Place SE and the Lake Sammamish State Park entrance, connecting the neighborhood with Central Issaquah, scheduled May 2028 to 2030. City of Issaquah

October 12, 2026

NW Sammamish Road is closed west of the state park until mid-November; homes inside the closure keep local access. Coming up: a volunteer planting party at Timberlake Park, about 24 forested acres with a half-mile trail to the beach, Sunday, November 15, 9 AM to noon. Park on 185th Avenue SE; 184th is private. Questions people ask

Is this a real estate newsletter?

No. It is a Issaquah newsletter that happens to know the housing market. Three of the four sections are about living here. The fourth tells you what homes are selling for, because in Issaquah that is news too.

Who writes it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publisher. Every item comes from Issaquah's own public record (council agendas, permit notices, school district and transit updates, parks calendars) and links its source. It is researched and written with AI tools, and an editor reads every issue before it goes out.

How often, and is it really free?

Once a week, Thursday at 6 AM, about five minutes to read. Free, no paywall, unsubscribe in one click at the bottom of any issue.

Who owns it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publishing company. The newsletter is free, and no sponsor buys coverage. Our standards.

What happens with my address?

It is used for one thing: a monthly estimate of what your home would sell for today. It goes to a local real estate brokerage under the same ownership as our publisher only if you tick the sharing box on the form; that brokerage may then contact you about your home. Leave the box empty and it stays with us. Never sold or posted. Remove it any time by replying to any issue.
